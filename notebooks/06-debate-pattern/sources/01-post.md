AGENTS THAT SEE - AGENTS THAT TALK - Post 6 of 15 - Patterns

Ask one smart model for its opinion and you get an answer polished to a shine - including the cracks it polished over. Now appoint two advocates who are forbidden to agree with each other, and a judge who must rule in a format a machine can read. The polish disappears. The cracks are the exhibit.

THE 30-SECOND VERSION
The debate pattern: a proponent builds the strongest case for, an opponent builds the strongest case against, and a judge hears both and returns a parseable verdict - PROCEED, PAUSE, or REJECT, with the strongest point each way. Advocates never concede. The tension is not a side effect; the tension is the instrument.

THE IDEA TO STEAL - DISAGREEMENT IS A SENSOR
A confidence score tells you how sure a model sounds. A debate shows you where the strongest arguments collide: where two advocates fight hardest and neither wins cleanly, the balance deserves a human's attention. Read the verdict, by all means - and file the map of the fight as a prompt for review. It is not a risk register, and it locates no genuine uncertainty: a real position needs independent evidence and counsel review.

HOW IT RUNS
- PROPONENT - the strongest case for, evidence first
- OPPONENT - the strongest case against, no alternatives offered
- JUDGE - hears both, rules in a fixed format
- Afterwards: audit the cost. Was the verdict worth the tokens?

APPLIED POC - LEGAL
Idea: Stress-test a contract position before a human negotiates it.
Build: A debate panel over a clause set: pro argues acceptability, con argues exposure, the judge returns a structured verdict for counsel.
Guardrail: The verdict informs counsel. It never accepts, signs, or advises a client.
The win: the strongest point each way, on the record, in a format counsel can audit. With scripted advocates the panel can only argue what it was given - the unconsidered argument is the reader's exercise, not this demo's claim.

WHERE IT BREAKS
- Advocates who never concede can entrench nonsense
- Judge quality caps the whole pattern
- Three agents spend roughly three passes over the material of one opinion

TRY THIS WEEK
Take one genuinely unresolved decision from your own work and run it through pro, con, judge. Keep the strongest losing argument in your project notes. In six months, check which side aged better - that is your calibration data.

Community: bring one genuinely unresolved decision. Would a debate have changed it?
